Chamber ramp & hold sizing

A parametric sizing envelope for the air-handling, radiant and plant capacity needed to ramp and hold a test chamber with a reconfigurable, climate-emulating facade. Every input is a live variable. The physics runs in Python on the server; this page only draws it.

Assumptions & limitations — read before you quote these numbers
  • Design capacity is the larger of the operating and ramp modes, never their sum. The room is either holding an experiment or ramping between two.
  • Ramp power assumes a constant-rate linear transition between setpoints, sized against the instantaneous load at the far end of the ramp. For a lumped capacity this is exact; tests/test_dynamic_reference.py confirms it against a time-stepped model. That far-end load uses the same extreme boundary as the operating mode, including real sun through glazing, but with nobody inside and the Artificial Sun off; only the share of equipment set by Equipment on during ramp still runs.
  • Hung radiant ceiling panels carry load up to their W/m² limit in both modes, and the air coil carries the rest; supply airflow is sized on that remainder. The panels hang clear of the structure, so they charge no mass directly and the surface-film limit is unchanged by them.
  • Thermal mass is diffusion-limited. Only the depth heat actually reaches within the ramp counts, using the closed-form linear-ramp solution for a semi-infinite solid. That assumes the surface itself follows the ramp perfectly, so it overstates participating mass; the real number is lower and the sizing is conservative.
  • The surface film is checked separately. Heat enters the mass through roughly 8 W/m²K of combined convection and radiation. If the air would have to run further from the surfaces than you allow, the ramp time is unreachable at any coil size.
  • Winter heating design ignores solar gain (conservative). Summer cooling includes it via SHGC × glazed area × irradiance.
  • Opaque surfaces are driven by air temperature only — no sol-air correction for absorbed radiation on the opaque facade or roof. At high irradiance this understates the cooling load.
  • The facade is the whole of the longest wall, split into glazed and opaque by the window-to-wall ratio. Only the floor and the three remaining walls are treated as adiabatic, with a small residual U-value against the surrounding lab. The facade and the ceiling are real boundaries and see the same emulated climate.
  • Latent load uses ASHRAE psychrometrics from dry-bulb and RH on both sides, checked against published table values. It does not model coil bypass, reheat, or the moisture stored in the room's own materials.
  • Heat-pump COP is Carnot times an efficiency factor, not a manufacturer curve. With the tanks on, the source is the tank temperature across the exchanger approach; with them off, it is the winter or summer design air across the outdoor coil approach. Neither the tanks' capacity nor the anergy network behind them is checked.
  • Dehumidification is reported separately and is not included in the cooling capacity.
  • Fan heat, duct gain, filter pressure drop and defrost energy are excluded. That is what the margin slider is for.
  • A first-pass exploration tool, not a substitute for a mechanical engineer's own load calculation and equipment selection.
How much of this is checked?

Every number above comes from src/zcbsl_resize/physics.py, which is covered by a test suite you can run with pytest. Psychrometrics are checked against published ASHRAE table values. The diffusion-limited mass model is checked against an independently written 1D finite-difference solve. The ramp equation is checked against a time-stepped two-node model of the chamber. See docs/validation.md for the worked hand-calculations.
